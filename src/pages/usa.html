US stock market overview

The S&P 500 covers about 500 large US companies. Meanwhile, the Nasdaq 100 tracks the largest non-financial companies listed on Nasdaq, and the Dow Jones follows 30 well-known blue-chip stocks. Together, they give a quick read on the US stock market. At the top of the page, you can switch between them and compare their moves over one month to several years.

How to follow the US stock market

First, look at the S&P 500 heatmap. Each block is a company, sized by its market value and coloured by its latest move, so it shows at a glance which sectors are leading. Next, the movers list shows the most active stocks, the top gainers and the top losers. For deeper research, the screener ranks US stocks and lets you filter by valuation, dividends, performance and technical rating. Finally, open any stock for its chart, financials, news and technical ratings.

About the data

US stock and ETF prices come from Nasdaq and NYSE through TradingView and are delayed. In contrast, index levels are FOREX.com CFD quotes that closely track the S&P 500, Nasdaq 100 and Dow Jones. In addition, the economic calendar lists US events such as jobs reports, inflation data and Federal Reserve decisions. For India, see the India page.
